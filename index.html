Network Geography

Each train line serves a many suburbs in its general region (North, West, South East, etc.) and brings passengers to the city for their final destination or point of transfer to other train lines. The interactive map below shows the daily passenger volume of all stations in the Melbourne metro network indicated by circles. As we can see, some of the most busiest lines on the network are those of the Burnley Group (Belgrave, Lilydale, Alamein, and Glen Waverly) and the Pakenham ans Cranbourne Lines. The map also highlights some regional hubs where passenger numbers are above average, which are South Yarra, Footscray, Caufield, Box Hill, and Dandenong among others.

Data collection ends 2025, as such it does not include the opening of the new Metro Tunnel stations (Anzac, Parkville, Arden) in late 2025.

When People Ride

The networks is stressed at two points throughout each day: the AM and PM Peaks, where commuters go to the city to work and school in the morning, and when they return again in the evening. Congestion is normal during these periods and as such Metro Trains has allocated more services to run during the periods of 7:00 AM to 9:30 AM and 4:00 PM to 6:00 PM. The brunt of this is seen on the Cranbourne, Pakenham, and Sunbury lines where peak periods see a train service every 3-5 minutes in the respective peak direction compared to other lines of 7-10 minutes.

Why People Ride

Passengers travel for different reasons such as work, education, and leisure among others, and these purposes shape where and when demand peaks. Understanding journey intent helps explain network patterns and reveals the role the metro network plays in the daily life of Melbournians.

Station Hubs & Recovery

Not all stations are equal. The City Loop stations form the network's backbone, acting as people movers in Melbourne's densest area while suburban hubs create dispersed demand centers, often as interchange points to nearby train lines.

City Loop: The Central Hub
Exit shares across the five CBD loop stations
Flinders Street and Southern Cross are the busiest stations on the network, historically and currently. Southern Cross itself is a major hub for commuters using the VLine, coming in from Melbourne's neighbouring regional areas. Flinders Street is the most centrally located station though, with almost all trains services starting and terminating there.
Busiest Suburban Stations
Demand peaks outside the central business district
Outside the City Loop, some suburban stations act as smaller hubs for passenger traffic. Footscray, for example, is the closest station in Melbourne's North with connections to other train lines, alongside being a dedicated stop on certain VLine train routes. Besides being very close to the Melbourne CBD, it is very affordable and is quickly growing (Kutschukian, 2025).

Reliability & Performance

To dutifully serve the people of Melbourne, the metro network must be reliable. Cancellations and Punctuality are key metrics to track, often interrelated, and are used to determine the quality of service provided to its commuters.

Most Cancelled Lines
Disruption rankings by service line
The Hurstbridge line faces the most cancellations with almost 2% of scheduled services cancelled. As the frequency of services are usually 10 minutes during peak periods and 30 minutes elsewhere, the impact for commuters is significant. The top 5 lines here are dominated by the Clifton Hill group in red, and the Pakenham and Subury lines (part of the new Caufield and Sunbury group).
On-Time Performance by Line
Punctuality tracking against the 92% operational target
Delays here are championed by the Glen Waverly and Sandringham lines, but the Dandenong corridor used by the Cranbourne and Pakenham lines both rank in the top 5. It is likely that delays are caused by congestion and knock-on effects from other lines, which is a common issue in metro networks worldwide.
